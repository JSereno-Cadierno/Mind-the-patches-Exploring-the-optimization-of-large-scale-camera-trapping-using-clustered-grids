# Mind the patches

Repository for data and code for the manuscript entitled: Mind the patches: Exploring the optimization of large-scale camera trap sampling through 
clustered grids by Jorge Sereno-Cadierno, Mizar Torrijo-Salesa, Roberto Pascual-Rico, Marta Monfort-Calatayud & Pelayo Acevedo, accepted for publication in Ecosphere.

### Abstract

An important challenge in large-scale camera-trap studies is to increase field efficiency without compromising the reliability and precision of commonly used ecological metrics. This challenge is particularly acute in large study areas, where logistical constraints limit sampling intensity and have motivated interest in alternative sampling designs, such as clustered camera-trap layouts. However, empirical evaluations of the effects of using clustered designs on common camera trap-derived metrics remain limited, especially in multispecies contexts.
We compared the performance of systematic and clustered camera-trap sampling designs in one of the largest protected areas in Iberia using standard metrics: trapping rate, activity, daily range, and population density. The effects of sampling effort were also explored across designs by simulating multiple camera densities by selecting smaller subsets of cameras. The analyses were conducted for ten mammal species (6 ungulates, 3 carnivores and 1 lagomorph) differing in spatial distribution and aggregation patterns.
Clustered designs substantially improved logistical efficiency, increasing deployment rates by approximately 89% compared to systematic designs. Differences in metric estimates and precision between designs were mediated by species’ spatial patterns. Estimates for non-aggregated species (in our study area: Cervus elaphus, Dama dama, Capreolus capreolus, Sus scrofa and Vulpes vulpes) were consistent across designs, whereas clustered layouts led to significant differences and reduced precision for some metrics in species with patched distributions.
Our results indicate that clustered camera-trap designs can markedly enhance field efficiency in large-scale monitoring programs without compromising the reliability of ecological metrics for common and moderately aggregated species but ask for care when working with patched and highly aggregated species in the territory, emphasizing the need to align sampling design choices with species ecology and spatial processes.

### Data

The allresultsCuencav2.1 file contain the camera trap parameters data as used in the manuscript to run the different models. For more information, see the paper.
